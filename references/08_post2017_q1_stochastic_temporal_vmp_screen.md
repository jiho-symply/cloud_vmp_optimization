# Post-2017 Q1/Top-Venue Screen for Stochastic Temporal VM Placement

## Claim

The post-2017 Q1/top-venue literature supports the current formulation as a careful synthesis, not as a direct copy of one prior model. I did not find a top-tier paper that simultaneously contains all of the following elements:

- VM-to-server placement formulated as temporal bin packing over fixed VM activity windows.
- Stochastic realized VM CPU or multi-resource usage while VMs are active.
- Two-stage scenario recourse with migration, spot/preemptible suspension, and managed batch processing/reservation.
- Energy-aware server activation over a finite planning horizon.

The strongest defensible claim is therefore conditional: temporal VMP/TBPP is a standard skeleton for time-windowed placement, stochastic/chance-constrained bin packing is a standard way to model uncertain realized resource usage, and recent production VM-allocation work shows that cloud operators do use temporal predictions and distributional information in placement decisions.

## How the Current Model Matches

The current model matches four post-2017 evidence streams.

First, the time-indexed placement skeleton is supported by temporal and dynamic bin-packing papers. Aydin et al. model cloud VM placement as temporal bin packing with VM start and end times. Dell'Amico et al. provide the general TBPP formulation and exact branch-and-price machinery. Buchbinder et al., Barbalho et al., and Ling et al. show that VM allocation in production-scale clouds is naturally viewed as dynamic bin packing, where lifetime or load information affects server fragmentation and busy time.

Second, stochastic realized resource usage is supported by stochastic VM placement, stochastic load balancing, and stochastic bin-packing papers. Zhou et al. and Chen et al. directly model VM CPU/memory requirement variation. Yu et al. connect stochastic VM workload, probabilistic overload guarantees, multidimensional resources, and migration. Yan et al. provide strong cloud-scale evidence for chance-constrained stochastic packing with dynamic resource usage. Hong et al. provide the closest theoretical support for stochastic item sizes that vary while jobs remain active.

Third, risk controls are supported by chance-constrained, CVaR, robust, and stochastic-programming references. Cohen et al. justify cloud overcommitment as bin packing with chance constraints. Zhang et al. formulate chance-constrained bin packing as two-stage stochastic and distributionally robust optimization. Rockafellar and Uryasev justify CVaR mathematically, and recent VM-placement security work shows that CVaR can be embedded into VMP analysis, although for a different risk source.

Fourth, the current model differs from the literature in a meaningful way. It combines several established abstractions in one compact MILP, but the exact combination of on-demand, spot, managed batch reservation, migration, suspension, scenario-dependent usage, and scalar CPU capacity is not directly validated by one paper.

## Different Modeling Choices in the Literature

The exact-match category is empty under the requested filter. No verified post-2017 Q1/top paper found here models the same combination of stochastic VM resource usage, temporal VM windows, two-stage recourse, migration, suspension, managed batch reservation, and energy-aware server activation.

The closest direct cloud stochastic-packing evidence is Yan et al. 2022 and Hong et al. 2023/2024. Yan et al. is operationally close because it is cloud-scale, chance-constrained, and based on dynamic resource usage distributions, but it schedules containers/services in daily batches rather than VMs with explicit activity windows. Hong et al. is mathematically close because item sizes change over time while jobs remain active, but it is an online service-system model rather than a finite-horizon placement MILP.

The closest direct stochastic VM-resource evidence is Zhou et al. 2019, Chen et al. 2020, and Yu et al. 2020. They support stochastic CPU/memory requirements, variation-aware feasibility, overload probabilities, migration, and SLA degradation through hotspots. They do not provide temporal bin-packing time windows.

The closest temporal VM-allocation evidence is Aydin et al. 2020, Buchbinder et al. 2021, Barbalho et al. 2023, and Ling et al. 2025. Aydin et al. provides the clean deterministic temporal VMP/TBPP mapping. Buchbinder et al., Barbalho et al., and Ling et al. show dynamic VM allocation with predictions in research and production systems. They support temporal uncertainty and fragmentation-aware placement, but not stochastic realized CPU usage in the same sense as chance-constrained SBPP.

The strongest stochastic-programming methodology is Zhang et al. 2020 plus Bulbul et al. 2021. Zhang et al. justifies two-stage chance-constrained bin packing with uncertain item sizes. Bulbul et al. justifies multi-stage stochastic cloud resource planning. Together they support the planning-plus-recourse abstraction, even though neither is a physical-server VMP paper.

Online stochastic placement is an alternative to the current model. Guo et al. and Gupta and Radovanovic support policies that adapt continuously to arrivals or demand rates. These are better references for rolling online schedulers than for a 24-hour two-stage plan.

Robust optimization is another alternative. It should be used when scenario probabilities are unreliable or when the operator wants worst-case protection. The cost is usually more active capacity and less aggressive consolidation.

## Caveats

Do not cite cloud batch stochastic bin packing as if it were VM placement. Yan et al. supports dynamic cloud resource usage distributions and chance constraints, but the objects are containers/services.

Do not cite lifetime-prediction VM allocation as if it modeled stochastic CPU usage. Barbalho et al. and Ling et al. are strong production evidence for temporal uncertainty and distributional forecasts, but their core uncertainty is lifetime, not realized resource size.

Do not cite stochastic bin packing alone as evidence for migration or spot suspension. Migration, suspension, and managed batch reservation are operational recourse choices that require separate justification.

Do not overstate CPU-only modeling. The strongest stochastic VM placement papers usually include CPU and memory, and some include network bandwidth, migration overhead, or topology.

Do not infer a fleet-level SLA from per-server or per-class chance constraints unless the mathematical formulation enforces the corresponding joint event or aggregate reliability target.

Screened-out but useful context: Kiessler et al. 2022 explicitly uses "stochastic temporal bin packing" for cloud portfolio allocation with reserved/on-demand/spot market types, but the verified venue is iiWAS/LNCS rather than the requested Q1/top tier. It can be mentioned as supplemental context only, not as primary evidence.

## Verdict

After widening the search to post-2017 Q1/top venues, the best available evidence supports the current model as a synthesis of established modeling families:

- Temporal VMP/TBPP supports the time-windowed placement skeleton.
- Stochastic VM placement and stochastic bin packing support random realized VM usage and probabilistic capacity feasibility.
- Chance constraints, CVaR, robust optimization, and stochastic programming support risk-aware planning under uncertain resource demand.
- Production VM-allocation papers support dynamic bin-packing views and the operational value of temporal predictions.

The strongest paper-positioning sentence is:

> Because no single prior model appears to combine temporal VMP, stochastic realized VM usage, and the proposed recourse structure, we build on temporal VM placement as bin packing, stochastic/chance-constrained bin packing for uncertain resource usage, and stochastic cloud resource planning for planning-plus-recourse decisions.
